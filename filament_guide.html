3D Printing Filament Guide

Compare common FDM filaments by ease of printing, strength, heat resistance, flexibility, and typical applications.

PLA

Easy

A popular all-purpose filament and one of the easiest materials for beginners.

✓ Pros

  • Very easy to print
  • Low warping
  • Excellent detail
  • Inexpensive

✕ Cons

  • Low heat resistance
  • Can be brittle
  • Not ideal outdoors
Typical uses: Models, prototypes, décor, display parts

PETG

Versatile

A durable material that offers a useful middle ground between PLA and more demanding engineering plastics.

✓ Pros

  • Good strength
  • Better heat resistance than PLA
  • Good chemical resistance
  • Relatively easy to print

✕ Cons

  • Can string
  • More difficult than PLA
  • Can have a glossy finish
Typical uses: Functional parts, brackets, containers, outdoor items

ABS

Engineering

A tough engineering plastic with good temperature resistance, but it requires more controlled printing conditions.

✓ Pros

  • Tough and impact resistant
  • Good heat resistance
  • Can be post-processed with acetone

✕ Cons

  • Significant warping
  • Needs good ventilation
  • Enclosure often recommended
Typical uses: Housings, automotive parts, tools, durable prototypes

ASA

Outdoor

Similar to ABS but generally better suited to outdoor applications because of its UV resistance.

✓ Pros

  • Excellent UV resistance
  • Good heat resistance
  • Strong and durable

✕ Cons

  • Can warp
  • Benefits from an enclosure
  • Requires ventilation
Typical uses: Outdoor parts, automotive components, equipment housings

Nylon

Tough

A strong, wear-resistant engineering filament useful for demanding functional components.

✓ Pros

  • Excellent toughness
  • High wear resistance
  • Good fatigue resistance

✕ Cons

  • Absorbs moisture quickly
  • Harder to print
  • Often needs drying
Typical uses: Gears, hinges, mechanical parts, fixtures

TPU

Flexible

A flexible elastomeric filament available in different hardness levels.

✓ Pros

  • Flexible and elastic
  • Good impact resistance
  • Good abrasion resistance

✕ Cons

  • Prints slowly
  • Can be difficult to feed
  • Stringing can occur
Typical uses: Gaskets, feet, seals, phone cases, flexible mounts

Polycarbonate

High Performance

A strong, stiff engineering material with high temperature resistance.

✓ Pros

  • Very strong
  • High heat resistance
  • Excellent impact resistance

✕ Cons

  • High printing temperatures
  • Prone to warping
  • Requires a capable printer
Typical uses: Engineering parts, fixtures, high-strength components

PLA+

Beginner

A family of modified PLA formulations designed to improve toughness or print performance.

✓ Pros

  • Easy to print
  • Often tougher than standard PLA
  • Good surface finish

✕ Cons

  • Properties vary by brand
  • Still limited by PLA heat resistance
Typical uses: Prototypes, models, general-purpose parts
Filament characteristics vary by formulation, brand, printer, and print settings.
